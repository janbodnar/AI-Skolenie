# OpenAI and GPT Models 

OpenAI is today the most famous artificial intelligence company in the world.  
It is the company behind the product that sparked a global wave of interest in AI –  
the chatbot **ChatGPT**. In 2022, it became the fastest growing  
consumer application in history and opened the era of modern language  
models to hundreds of millions of ordinary users.  

## Founding of the Company and the Founders  

OpenAI was founded in December **2015** as a non-profit organization based  
in San Francisco. Its founding mission reads: *„to ensure that artificial  
general intelligence (AGI) benefits all of humanity."*  

Among the eleven founders were names that resonate across the industry today:  

*   **Sam Altman (CEO):** Until then chairman of the prestigious startup  
    accelerator Y Combinator. He became the face and the controversial center  
    of the company.
*   **Elon Musk (co-chair):** Co-founder of Tesla and SpaceX, who  
    provided the initial funding and media attention. In 2018, he left  
    the board of directors
    due to a conflict of interest with AI development at Tesla.  
*   **Greg Brockman (president):** Technical co-founder and long-time  
    pillar of the company,
    architect of the corporate culture.  
*   **Ilya Sutskever (chief scientist):** One of the world's foremost  
    deep learning researchers,
    key to training GPT models. In 2024, he left  
    and founded his own AI safety company.  

The company secured investment commitments of **1 billion dollars**, but in reality  
it raised
significantly less – according to later reports only about 130 million by 2019.  
Despite this, in its early years it attracted top researchers who left  
Google, Facebook, and other tech giants.  

### From Non-Profit to For-Profit Structure  

In 2019, OpenAI created a **capped-profit** subsidiary
(limited profit). Investors could earn at most 100 times their stake;
the rest was to remain with the non-profit foundation. In October 2025, the main commercial
part was reorganized into a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation. The non-profit
organization OpenAI Foundation remained part of the governance structure.

Microsoft remains a significant partner and investor, but the exact stake and its
economic rights depend on the terms of the reorganization. In February 2026,
OpenAI announced funding of **110 billion dollars** at a valuation of approximately
730 billion dollars; these are announced, not audited, figures.

## Sam Altman – a Controversial Figure  

Sam Altman is one of the most prominent, yet also most controversial  
figures
in the entire tech industry. Some celebrate him as a visionary, others  
see him as a pragmatist for whom principles are subordinate to ambition.  

### Dismissal and Dramatic Return (November 2023)  

The events of November 2023 are unprecedented in the history of Silicon Valley.  
**On 17 November 2023,**
the board consisting of Helen Toner, Ilya Sutskever, Adam D'Angelo and  
Tasha McCauley
removed Altman as CEO with the justification that he *„had not been  
consistently open in his communications."* Within an hour of the announcement, a frantic  
behind-the-scenes battle began.

Microsoft immediately offered Altman leadership of a new AI team. OpenAI  
employees
responded: **738 of 770 workers** signed an open letter threatening  
mass resignation and a move to Microsoft if the board did not step down  
and bring Altman back. Ilya Sutskever, who originally voted for the dismissal,  
publicly apologized on X with the words: *„I regret my participation in  
the board's actions."*  

Four days later, on **21 November 2023**, Altman triumphantly returned  
to the position of CEO.
The original board was replaced by new members led by Bret Taylor  
(ex-chairman of Twitter and co-CEO of Salesforce).  

### Disputes and Conflicts of Interest  

Altman's investment portfolio spans **400 companies** worth around  
2.8 billion dollars – many of them with business relationships with OpenAI,  
which raises questions about conflicts of interest.  

In 2024, Elon Musk filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Altman, claiming the company  
had betrayed its founding mission of benefiting humanity. OpenAI responded  
that Musk had promised to invest 1 billion, but in reality put in only 45 million  
and suggested merging OpenAI with Tesla or taking control of it.  


### Political Engagement and Ambitions  

Altman is among the most active political lobbyists in the AI space.  
In 2023, he completed a global tour, during which he met  
22 heads of state and government. Time ranked him among the „Architects of AI" and named  
him Person of the Year 2025.

in the modern

to developers and the public.

OpenAI's model numbering is divided into the general GPT family, reasoning variants,
Codex models, and smaller *mini* or *nano* versions. The model label in ChatGPT
may not be identical to the model name in the API. GPT-5.6 has three permanent performance
levels: **Sol**, **Terra**, and **Luna**. The number denotes the generation, while the name
denotes the performance and price tier.

### GPT-5.6 Terra, Luna, and Sol

**GPT-5.6 Sol** is the flagship version for complex tasks, long agentic  
workflows, coding, scientific work, cybersecurity, and computer use.  
It is available in ChatGPT, Codex, and the OpenAI API. In ChatGPT, there is also a variant  
**Sol Pro** for the highest quality on demanding tasks.  
  
**GPT-5.6 Terra** is a balanced version for everyday professional work.  
It offers performance roughly at the level of GPT-5.5 at a lower price and is suitable for  
document analysis, coding, search, and routine agentic workflows.  
  
**GPT-5.6 Luna** is the fastest and most economical version. It is designed for  
simpler tasks, large volumes of requests, and applications sensitive to price  
or latency. In the API, it has the lowest price in the GPT-5.6 family.  
  
According to OpenAI's official price list, the base prices per 1 million tokens are:  
  
| Version | Input | Output | Typical use |  
| :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- |  
| **GPT-5.6 Sol** | 5 $ | 30 $ | Highest quality and complex tasks |  
| **GPT-5.6 Terra** | 2.50 $ | 15 $ | Balanced performance and price |  
| **GPT-5.6 Luna** | 1 $ | 6 $ | Speed, volume, and low cost |  
  
OpenAI may change the availability of individual tiers depending on the product, plan, and  
load. In the API, all three tiers are available separately; in ChatGPT Work  
and Codex, users can choose Sol, Terra, or Luna according to their permissions.  

### GPT-3: The First Real Breakthrough  

GPT-3 from 2020 was an electric shock to the industry. With 
**175 billion parameters**
and training on a huge amount of internet texts, it could write essays,  
translate languages, answer questions, and generate code – without any  
additional fine-tuning. Developers who gained access via the API  
published demos that went viral on social media.  

> GPT-3 is considered the historic turning point – the moment LLM models  
> became practically usable tools, not just scientific experiments.  

### GPT-3.5 and ChatGPT: A Boom for the Masses  

In November 2022, OpenAI launched **ChatGPT** – a simple chat interface  
for GPT-3.5 aimed at ordinary users. The result was unprecedented:  

*   **1 million users in 5 days** – no application in history  
    achieved this faster.
*   **100 million users in 2 months** – ChatGPT became  
    the fastest growing
    consumer application ever (Instagram took 2.5 years,
    TikTok 9 months).  
*   In February 2026, according to publicly reported figures, ChatGPT had  
    approximately **900 million weekly active users**.  

The launch of ChatGPT kicked off the so-called **AI boom** – Google, Meta, Microsoft  
and dozens
of startups immediately accelerated research and development of their own models.  

### GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.6 Sol: The Current Generation  

**GPT-5.4** was released in March 2026 in ChatGPT as GPT-5.4 Thinking,
in the API as `gpt-5.4` and `gpt-5.4-pro`, and in Codex. It combines reasoning, coding,
professional work, and agentic workflows. It is OpenAI's first general model
with native computer-use support in the API and Codex; the standard
API interface supports a context of up to **272,000 tokens**, while Codex had
an experimental mode of up to **1 million tokens**.

The main new features include **tool search**, more efficient agentic tool calling,
better web search, work with spreadsheets and presentations,
as well as more precise control of the graphical interface via mouse and keyboard.
GPT-5.4 also includes the coding capabilities of GPT-5.3-Codex.

In July 2026, OpenAI began making **GPT-5.6 Sol** available in ChatGPT as a
flagship reasoning model for complex tasks in coding, research, science,
cybersecurity, computer use, and design. Availability depends on the
plan and gradual rollout. **GPT-5.5** remains part of the newer branch of
Instant/Thinking models; its exact product deployment may differ
from the names used in the API.

## ChatGPT for the Public vs. the OpenAI Platform  

This is one of the most important practical distinctions worth  
understanding.

### ChatGPT (chatgpt.com)  

ChatGPT is a **consumer application** designed for ordinary users.  
You access it through a web browser or a mobile app; it requires  
no programming knowledge. You just type a question and get an answer.  

| Plan | Price | Contents |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **Free** | free | Limited access to current models and tools |  
| **Plus** | 20 $/mo. | Higher limits, reasoning, and advanced tools |  
| **Pro** | 200 $/mo. | Highest limits and access to Pro models |  
| **Team / Enterprise** | custom price | Team management, company data, SLA |  
|  |  | contracts |

ChatGPT is designed for **direct conversation** – writing texts, answering  
questions, analyzing documents, generating images, searching the web.  
Conversation happens through the web interface; no programming is required.  

### OpenAI Platform (platform.openai.com)  

The OpenAI Platform is a **developer environment** for companies and programmers who  
want to integrate GPT models into their own applications, products, and systems.  

Access works via the **REST API** – a developer sends an HTTP request with text  
(prompt) and receives a response in JSON format. Payment is based on the consumption  
of tokens, i.e., units of text processed by the model.  

Key platform features:  

*   **API calls** – integration of GPT models into any application.  
*   **Fine-tuning** – customizing models on your own company data.  
*   **Responses API** – modern interface for models, tools, and agents.  
*   **Assistants API** – older interface that OpenAI is gradually replacing.  
*   **Batch API** – bulk processing of thousands of requests at once for  
    a lower price.
*   **Embeddings** – converting text to vectors for search and recommendations.  
*   **Moderations API** – automatic detection of inappropriate content.  

> **In simple terms:** ChatGPT is like a ready-made app in a store.  
> The OpenAI Platform is like an SDK and a parts store – a developer  
> builds their own application from them as needed.  

## The OpenAI Python Library – A De Facto Standard  

While theoretically you can communicate with the OpenAI API through any HTTP client,  
in practice the **official Python library `openai`** has become the de facto standard  
for integrating AI models in all languages and frameworks.  

### Why Is It a De Facto Standard?  

*   **Simplicity:** A few lines of code are enough for full integration.  
*   **Documentation:** The best-covered and most extensive documentation  
    in the industry.
*   **Availability:** Installation with a single command `pip install openai`.  
*   **Compatibility:** Most other providers (Anthropic, Grok,  
    Mistral, local models via Ollama) offer **compatible APIs** –  
    i.e., the same calls
    work with their models too, you just change the `base_url`.  
*   **Widespread use:** It is integrated into thousands of frameworks, tools,  
    and open-source projects (LangChain, LlamaIndex, AutoGPT, etc.).  

### Basic Usage – The Responses API  

In March 2025, OpenAI introduced the modern **Responses API**  
(`client.responses.create`),
which replaced the older Chat Completions and brings a simpler interface  
with native support for tools, stateful context, and agents.  

```python  
from openai import OpenAI  

client = OpenAI(api_key="sk-...")  # API key from platform.openai.com  

response = client.responses.create(  
    model="gpt-5.4",  
    instructions="You are a helpful assistant.",  
    input="Explain to me what an LLM is."  
)  

print(response.output_text)  
```  

Compared to the older interface, the code is significantly shorter – `instructions` replaces  
the system message and `input` is the text of the question. The answer is directly in  
`response.output_text`.

The Responses API also natively supports **tools** directly in a single call:  

```python  
# Web search without additional configuration  
response = client.responses.create(  
    model="gpt-5.4",  
    tools=[{"type": "web_search_preview"}],  
    input="What are the latest AI models released in March 2026?"  
)  

print(response.output_text)  
```  

### Compatibility with Other Providers  

This is the fundamental reason why the openai library is so dominant –  
many other providers accept it with a compatible API.  
The compatibility applies to the older **Chat Completions** interface,  
which is still fully functional and which other providers implement:  

```python  
# The same code works, for example, with a local model via Ollama  
client = OpenAI(base_url="http://localhost:11434/v1", api_key="ollama")  
response = client.chat.completions.create(  
    model="llama3",  
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "What is an LLM?"}]  
)  
print(response.choices[0].message.content)  
```  

A programmer thus learns one interface and can use it  
with dozens of different models from different providers.

## Finances and Growth  

OpenAI's financial growth is one of the most dramatic stories in business  
history:

*   **2022:** Annual revenue under 30 million dollars.  
*   **2024:** 3.7 billion dollars, driven by ChatGPT subscriptions.  
*   **2025:** 13.1 billion dollars (4x growth in a single year).  
*   **2026:** Annualized revenue exceeded 20 billion dollars (January 2026).  

Despite the huge revenue, the company is still losing money –  
the estimated loss for 2025 is **9 billion dollars**, primarily due to  
astronomical costs of computing power (compute). OpenAI plans  
to spend around **600 billion dollars** on infrastructure by 2030.  

### Why Do Investors Fund a Company That Is Losing Money?  

This is a question that stumps many people. The answer lies in the logic of **investing  
in the infrastructure of the future** – not in the ordinary business logic of profit and loss.  

**1. Betting on the category winner**  
Investors believe AI will be a technology comparable to electricity or  
the internet – and whoever gains a dominant position today will monetize it  
for decades. That is why it makes sense for SoftBank, Amazon, or Nvidia to invest  
tens of billions today, even though OpenAI is losing money – because the alternative (not investing  
and missing out on a share of the winning platform) is much worse.  

**2. Loss-making leaders are common in technology**  
Amazon lost money for 20 years (1994–2014) and today is one of  
the most valuable companies in the world. Uber, Airbnb, Spotify – they all  
lost money for years and today either profit or are valued in the billions.  
OpenAI is doing the same: instead of immediate profit, it invests in growth,  
research, and infrastructure.  

**3. Compute is strategic infrastructure**  
OpenAI's costs are primarily for **GPU clusters and energy** – i.e., physical  
infrastructure. These assets have long-term value regardless of  
which model is currently running. From the investors' point of view, they are buying a share not only in software,  
but also in the AI infrastructure of the coming decade.  

**4. Revenue grows faster than losses**  
From a trend perspective, the situation is different than it might seem:  

| Year | Revenue | Loss | Loss coverage by revenue |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| 2023 | ~1.6B $ | ~5B $ | 32 % |  
| 2024 | 3.7B $ | ~5B $ | 74 % |  
| 2025 | 13.1B $ | ~9B $ | 145 % |  

Revenue grows exponentially, while losses grow more moderately.  
Analysts expect the break-even point to be crossed around  
**2029**.

**5. Valuation reflects the future, not the present**  
A valuation of 730 billion dollars (February 2026) does not correspond to current revenue –  
it reflects what investors *expect*. Whoever owns 1 % of OpenAI today for  
7.3 billion,
is betting that the company will be worth trillions. It is speculation,  
but informed speculation backed by real growth.
